# ESCIE ANIMATED SERIES — MASTER BIBLE

Status: DEVELOPMENT / PRIVATE-SAFE ARCHITECTURE
Purpose: build the complete series system without publishing unreleased song, lyric, case-file, or Central material.

## 1. CORE IDENTITY

The animated series is not a separate universe.

It is another medium for the same ESCIE world:

LYRICS → STORY → EPISODES → ANIMATED SERIES

The public World remains the lived/observed layer.
The music remains the source material.
The series becomes the narrative interpretation layer.
The game remains the playable layer and is currently paused.

The series must never contradict the World, but it may reveal a different angle on the same events.

## 2. CORE QUESTIONS

WHO MADE HIM?
WHAT DID HE BECOME?
WHAT WILL HE MAKE?

These are the series spine.

The series does not need to answer every question immediately. Some answers should arrive as consequences, memories, relationships, objects, locations, and repeated images.

## 3. STORY RULE

Lyrics establish source material.

The adaptation pipeline must separate:

- CANON — directly established by approved source material.
- INFERENCE — strongly supported by multiple source details.
- ADAPTATION — created to connect scenes, characters, timing, or visual storytelling.
- UNKNOWN — intentionally unresolved.

Unknown is a valid story state.

Never manufacture evidence merely to close a mystery.

## 4. NARRATIVE ENGINE

Every episode should contain:

1. PRESENT ACTION
2. PRESSURE
3. MEMORY / INTERRUPTION
4. CHOICE
5. CONSEQUENCE
6. NEW QUESTION

The audience should be able to understand the immediate scene without understanding the entire mythology.

## 5. SEASON SHAPE

The first season follows transformation rather than a conventional biography.

ACT I — ORIGIN OF THE SIGNAL
- establish the person before the legend
- establish Montréal as lived environment
- introduce family, poverty, survival, relationships and pressure
- establish recurring objects and visual motifs

ACT II — THE MONSTER
- show behavior and consequence rather than simply labeling a person
- reveal the distance between self-image and outside perception
- use memory to expose conditioning
- allow contradictions to remain

ACT III — THE FATHER
- responsibility collides with inheritance
- protection and danger can occupy the same scene
- children change the meaning of earlier choices
- the question becomes what gets passed forward

ACT IV — THE HUMAN
- consequences accumulate
- evidence and memory stop agreeing cleanly
- the person, father, monster and myth occupy the same body
- the season ends with a deeper question rather than a clean moral resolution

## 6. EPISODE MODEL

Each episode has:

- episode ID
- working title
- source references
- chronology position
- primary character
- secondary characters
- locations
- objects
- events
- conflict
- emotional turn
- visual language
- recurring symbols
- sound language
- dialogue requirements
- canon facts
- inference
- adaptation decisions
- unknowns
- ending image
- next-question hook

Default episode length:
- short-form: 6–12 minutes
- standard: 15–24 minutes
- event episode: 25–35 minutes

Length is chosen by story density, not by platform convention.

Every major character receives:

IDENTITY

- age range
- relationship to ESCIE
- public-facing role
- private pressure

INNER ENGINE

- need

RELATIONSHIP ENGINE

VOICE ENGINE

No character is created solely to explain lore.

## 8. ESCIE CHARACTER MODEL

ESCIE is treated as a person with multiple perceived facets:

THE FATHER
THE MAN
THE MONSTER

The series should not turn these into three separate people unless approved source material explicitly requires it.

They are different pressures, behaviors, memories and interpretations occupying the same life.

The audience should sometimes disagree with ESCIE's own interpretation.

## 9. LOCATION SYSTEM

Every recurring location receives:

- physical identity
- time-of-day identity
- season/weather identity
- sound identity
- social identity
- memory association
- recurring objects
- recurring characters
- story function
- transformation across the season

Montréal remains the geographic center.

Locations should feel inhabited, not like backdrops.

## 10. SYMBOL SYSTEM

Symbols are narrative devices, not decoration.

A recurring symbol can carry:
- memory
- warning
- inheritance
- absence
- protection
- violence
- surveillance
- childhood
- time
- identity

Existing ESCIE visual motifs can migrate into animation when the story earns them.

Potential recurring visual languages include:
- camera / surveillance
- child-related objects
- nightlight
- key
- chair
- VHS / damaged media
- religious imagery
- Montréal winter
- concrete / fluorescent interiors
- written evidence
- photographs / negatives
- empty spaces

A symbol must gain meaning through repetition and change.

## 11. VISUAL LANGUAGE

Animation style changes with narrative state.

GROUNDED PRESENT
- cinematic 2.5D/3D
- realistic Montréal environments
- restrained camera movement
- documentary composition

MEMORY
- hand-drawn imperfections
- altered frame cadence
- incomplete backgrounds
- selective detail

TRAUMA
- rotoscope / damaged-media influence
- unstable framing
- compression artifacts
- visual discontinuity

INTERNAL THOUGHT
- surreal transitions
- impossible spatial relationships
- symbolic objects
- sound-led cuts

MYTH
- heightened composition
- deliberate repetition
- historical / archival textures

SURVEILLANCE
- fixed cameras
- distant lenses
- timestamps only when narratively justified
- no gratuitous UI

The visual medium itself communicates the reliability and emotional temperature of a scene.

## 12. CAMERA LANGUAGE

The camera has rules.

OBSERVE — distant, patient, documentary.
FOLLOW — handheld or body-level movement.
REMEMBER — unstable or selective.
CONFRONT — compressed space, closer framing.
CONFESS — minimal movement.
MYTHOLOGIZE — composed, symbolic, deliberate.
SURVEIL — fixed viewpoint.

Do not use constant cinematic movement merely because animation permits it.

## 13. EDITING LANGUAGE

Transitions should be motivated by:
- object match
- sound match
- repeated gesture
- location
- lyric phrase
- memory trigger
- visual symbol
- contradiction

Avoid exposition montages that simply explain the plot.

## 14. SOUND SYSTEM

Sound is a second narrator.

Layers:
1. dialogue
2. environment
3. body sounds
4. object sounds
5. city
6. music
7. silence

Silence is a production element.

Songs can appear as:
- source music
- diegetic playback
- memory fragments
- end-credit pieces
- thematic reprises

Never force a song into a scene just because the episode references it.

## 15. STORY ↔ MUSIC RULE

The lyrics are not automatically dialogue.

A lyric can become:
- action
- visual metaphor
- internal thought
- dialogue
- environmental detail
- title
- recurring motif
- episode structure

Literal adaptation is only used when it improves the story.

## 16. TIMELINE ENGINE

Maintain three timelines:

EVENT TIME — when something happened.
MEMORY TIME — when a character remembers it.
AUDIENCE TIME — when the viewer learns it.

These timelines do not need to match.

Every major reveal must record which timeline changed.

## 17. MYSTERY ENGINE

Questions should be generated from existing contradictions.

For each unresolved thread:
- question
- known facts
- conflicting facts
- who knows
- who believes they know
- evidence
- missing evidence
- next possible reveal
- permanent unknown status

No artificial cliffhangers.

## 18. EPISODE ENDINGS

Preferred endings:

IMAGE — a final object/location/person reframes the episode.
CONSEQUENCE — an action creates the next problem.
REVELATION — a known fact gains a new meaning.
QUESTION — a contradiction becomes visible.
SILENCE — the audience is left with an emotionally complete image.

## 19. SEASON CONTINUITY

A continuity ledger tracks:
- people
- ages
- relationships
- locations
- dates
- objects
- injuries
- possessions
- recurring phrases
- unresolved questions
- visual motifs
- source references

Nothing can silently change between episodes.

## 21. QUALITY GATES

SOURCE GATE
Every canon claim has an approved source.

STORY GATE
Every scene changes character, information, pressure, or consequence.

CONTINUITY GATE
No contradiction with approved canon.

VISUAL GATE
Every visual choice belongs to the ESCIE language.

SOUND GATE
Music and silence serve the scene.

DISCLOSURE GATE
Unreleased music, lyrics, private case files and Central material stay private.

PUBLIC-SITE GATE
The public site may reference the existence of the series only when deliberately approved. Development material does not automatically become public canon.

## 22. CROSS-MEDIA RELATIONSHIP

SONGS
↓
STORY
↓
ANIMATED SERIES
↓
ESCIE WORLD
↓
GAME

Information does not have to flow in only one direction.

A released story can deepen a World record.
A World discovery can change how a later episode is interpreted.
A game encounter can provide another perspective without rewriting canon.

The canon source remains controlled.
