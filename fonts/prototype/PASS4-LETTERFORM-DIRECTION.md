# ESCIE Typography — Letterform Pass 4

Status: **DESIGN PASS — PROTOTYPE ONLY**

This pass turns the existing prototype review into a concrete letterform acceptance system. It does not promote any font to the public site.

## 1. Primary objective

The ESCIE face must stop reading as a generic geometric/gothic construction and start reading as an authored identity.

The test is:

> Remove the word ESCIE. The letterforms should still feel like ESCIE.

Do not use distortion, glow, scanlines, texture, or color to manufacture identity. The identity must exist in the glyph construction itself.

## 2. ESCIE — identity face

### Proportions
- Tall, narrow capitals.
- Strong vertical presence.
- Moderate-to-tight sidebearings.
- Large, deliberate counters so the face survives phone sizes.
- No uniformly mechanical grid rhythm.

### Stroke language
- Heavy primary strokes.
- Slightly lighter secondary/diagonal movement.
- Terminals may be interrupted or beveled, but interruptions must feel structural.
- Damage is controlled: never random chips, horror scratches, spikes, or decorative gore.

### Human asymmetry
Asymmetry belongs in selected joins and terminals rather than every glyph. The family should feel designed by a person, not algorithmically corrupted.

### Signature glyphs
These receive individual art direction before the rest of the alphabet is considered finished:

- **A:** distinctive apex, compressed counter, slightly imperfect crossbar.
- **C:** open architectural mouth with unequal terminal tension.
- **E:** three horizontal movements with a visibly different middle arm.
- **G:** controlled opening plus an internal bar that feels proprietary.
- **M:** deep central descent; the middle should create an unmistakable ESCIE silhouette.
- **O:** narrow vertical oval rather than a generic circle.
- **R:** strong bowl-to-leg transition with a decisive diagonal.
- **S:** asymmetrical upper/lower tension.
- **T:** heavy top bar with a narrow authoritative stem.

These glyphs are the identity anchors. Effects are secondary.

## 3. ESCIE SIGNAL

SIGNAL is not a separate aesthetic.

It is ESCIE in an operational state.

- Preserve the same vertical proportions and counter philosophy.
- Reduce terminal damage.
- Reduce contrast and visual drama.
- Increase consistency of rhythm.
- Optimize for navigation, metadata, evidence labels and small phone text.
- Never become a generic monospace substitute.

The relationship should be obvious when ESCIE and SIGNAL are placed beside each other.

## 4. ESCIE SCRIPT

SCRIPT is the intimate state of the same system.

- Genuine handwritten movement.
- Human pressure and rhythm.
- Connected-feel strokes without forced calligraphic flourishes.
- Unevenness should come from movement, not random wobble.
- Avoid wedding/calligraphy/romantic-script associations.
- Lowercase is the priority.
- At small sizes, individual letters must remain distinguishable.

## 5. Family DNA

All three faces must share:

1. vertical proportion language;
2. terminal logic;
3. counter philosophy;
4. spacing discipline;
5. restrained asymmetry.

They differ by state:

**ESCIE = identity**  
**SIGNAL = operation**  
**SCRIPT = intimacy**

## 7. Rejection criteria

Reject the face if:

- it looks like a pixel font;
- it looks like a stock gothic font;
- it depends on effects to feel distinctive;
- the three faces look unrelated;
- French accents look bolted on;
- lowercase becomes decorative rather than readable;
- mobile rendering collapses counters or joins;
- the identity can be replicated simply by changing a normal font's weight/spacing.

## 8. Production boundary

The public site remains on the safe system-font stack.

The prototype family is not production typography until the monochrome desktop + phone specimen passes visual review and the final binaries pass the existing provenance, glyph, metadata and site-load gates.

**Next implementation target:** refine the signature ESCIE glyphs first, then rebuild the family around those anchors rather than adding more global effects.
