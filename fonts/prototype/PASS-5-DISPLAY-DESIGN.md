# ESCIE Typography Pass 5 — Organic Gothic Identity

Status: prototype only. Production typography remains untouched.

## Objective

Refine the Pass 4 architecture into a more human, narrow and authoritative ESCIE face.

Pass 5 prioritizes:
- organic rhythm over geometric perfection
- narrower proportions
- selective weight rather than uniform strokes
- asymmetric counters and curves
- restrained pointed terminals
- damaged elegance through construction, not texture
- phone readability at display and navigation sizes

## Signature glyphs

A — compressed counter, tall apex, restrained shoulders.
E — narrow stem with unequal horizontal authority.
G — controlled opening with a compact internal bar.
R — compact bowl and decisive descending leg.
S — upper/lower asymmetry with different pressure.
M — architectural descent without decorative spikes.
T — heavy horizontal authority over a narrow stem.
C — controlled opening and restrained terminals.
O — tall, narrow counter with a clean silhouette.

## What changed from Pass 4

Pass 4 established the architectural skeleton.

Pass 5 deliberately removes some of the rigid geometry:
- narrower stems
- less uniform curve weight
- asymmetrical S
- more compact bowls
- reduced terminal size
- slightly irregular proportions
- cleaner negative space

The goal is for the face to feel drawn by a person, not generated from a mechanical stencil.

## Anti-generic rules

No:
- blackletter imitation
- fantasy-rune construction
- random spikes
- cracks or fake damage
- decorative glitching
- copied reference outlines
- effects compensating for weak letterforms

## Acceptance gate

Pass 5 is not production-approved until:
1. identity reads unmistakably ESCIE
2. signature glyphs form a coherent family
3. phone readability survives
4. French/English coverage passes
5. provenance remains original
6. desktop specimen passes
7. phone specimen passes
8. user visually approves

Technical green is not creative approval.
